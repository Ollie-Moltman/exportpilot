1. Mandatory Documents (Every Indian Export)

These 6 documents are required for virtually every export shipment from India, regardless of product or destination:

1. IEC (Import Export Code)

MANDATORY

Your 10-digit DGFT-issued license number (e.g., AABCA1234E). Every Indian exporter MUST have an IEC. No IEC = no export.

Who issues: DGFT (dgft.gov.in) · Cost: Free · Time: 1-5 days

Pro tip: You need separate IECs for different registered offices if you're an entity with multiple branches.

2. Commercial Invoice

MANDATORY

The seller's "bill." Shows the transaction value, description, quantity, unit price, and total amount. The buyer's customs authority uses this to assess duty.

Must include: IEC number, buyer's details, product description with HS code, FOB/CIF value, payment terms, port of loading/discharge

3. Packing List

MANDATORY

Details every carton/package: number of packages, gross/net weight, dimensions, marking (shipping marks), and contents breakdown. Customs uses this to verify the shipment.

Difference from invoice: Invoice shows VALUE. Packing list shows WHAT'S INSIDE and HOW IT'S PACKED. Both are always required separately.

4. Bill of Lading (B/L) or Airway Bill (AWB)

MANDATORY

The "title document" — proof that your goods have been handed to the carrier for shipping. The buyer needs this to take delivery at the destination port.

Ocean Bill of Lading: For sea freight. Can be "straight" (non-negotiable) or "to order" (negotiable — endorsed on back to transfer title). · AWB: For air freight. Always non-negotiable.

Issued by: Shipping line or airline (through your freight forwarder)

5. Bill of Entry (Export)

MANDATORY

Filed by your CHA (Customs House Agent) at the Indian port of export. This is India's customs export declaration — proves the goods have legally exited India.

Filed at: Port of loading (JNPT, Mundra, Chennai, etc.) · Who files: Your CHA or freight forwarder on your behalf

Note: This is different from the buyer's "Bill of Entry" (import declaration) at the destination.

6. Shipping Bill / Export General Manifest (EGM)

MANDATORY

The Shipping Bill is the main export customs document in India — generated after EGM filing. It contains your IEC, shipping details, invoice value, and customs stamp of "Let Export" (clearance to ship).

No "Let Export" stamp: Your goods cannot be loaded onto the vessel. This is the final Indian customs clearance step.

3. FTA Documents — How to Claim Preferential Duty

If you're exporting to a country India has a Free Trade Agreement with (UAE, ASEAN, Korea, Japan, etc.), you can claim 0% or reduced customs duty using an FTA Certificate of Origin. This is where Indian exporters lose the most money — by not claiming FTAs they qualify for.

✅ Certificate of Origin (Preferential) — The Key FTA Document

This is a special CoO that states your product qualifies under a specific FTAs rules of origin. Without this, you pay full duty. With this, you pay 0% (for UAE CEPA on most products).

Major FTAs Indian Exporters Should Know

✓UAE CEPA — 0% duty on 90%+ of Indian goods (in effect May 2022)
✓ASEAN-India FTA — Reduced duties on goods to Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia, Malaysia, etc.
✓India-Korea CEPA — Reduced tariffs on goods to South Korea
✓India-Japan CEPA — Reduced duties on goods to Japan
✓India-Australia ECTA/ITA — Preferential access for many goods
✓UK-India FTA — Negotiations ongoing (check DGFT for latest status)

4. Typical Export Document Workflow

Here's the sequence Indian exporters follow for documentation:

1

Before Shipment (5-10 days prior)

Confirm order → Issue Proforma Invoice → Buyer arranges LC or payment → Apply for IEC if not done

2

Pre-Shipment (2-3 days prior)

Book freight with forwarder → Prepare Commercial Invoice + Packing List → Arrange product inspections/certifications if needed

3

At Port of Loading (Day of Shipment)

Hand over goods to carrier → CHA files Bill of Entry (export) → Customs examines if selected → "Let Export" stamp obtained → Bill of Lading issued → Shipping Bill filed

4

After Shipment

Send originals to buyer via bank (if LC) or courier → File GST returns (export is 0% GST with LUT) → Track shipment → Buyer clears goods at destination

5. Document Mistakes That Cost Indian Exporters Money

✗
HS code mismatch between documents — If your Commercial Invoice says HS 0904.11 but your COO says 0904.19, customs will flag it. All documents must use consistent codes.
✗
Incorrect FOB value — Under-invoicing to reduce duty is illegal. Over-invoicing happens accidentally. Both cause problems. Use the actual transaction value always.
✗
Shipping Bill filed with wrong port — The shipping bill must match the actual port of loading. If your goods move from Mumbai to JNPT for loading, the shipping bill shows JNPT as the port.
✗
Not sending originals of B/L to buyer in time — Your buyer cannot take delivery without the original Bill of Lading. If you're using an LC, send via your bank. For TT payments, courier immediately after loading.
✗
Missing Certificate of Origin when FTA-eligible — This is the most expensive mistake. You're paying full duty when you should be paying 0%. UAE CEPA alone could save you ₹50,000-5 lakhs per shipment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I export from India without a shipping bill?

No. The Shipping Bill is a mandatory document filed with Indian customs at the port of export. Without it, your goods cannot be legally exported. Even samples need a shipping bill (under a different category).

What's the difference between a Commercial Invoice and a Proforma Invoice?

A Proforma Invoice is a preliminary document sent before the sale is finalized — it acts as a quote. The Commercial Invoice is the actual tax invoice issued after the goods are shipped. Only the Commercial Invoice has legal/ customs value.

Who pays for Certificate of Origin certification?

Chambers of Commerce charge a fee — typically ₹100-500 per CoO depending on the value of the shipment. FIEO charges for preferential CoOs (for FTAs). ExportPilot generates the document for free; you only pay the certification fee at the Chamber.

Do I need a Customs House Agent (CHA) to export?

Not legally required, but strongly recommended for first-time exporters. A good CHA handles all customs paperwork, coordinates with port authorities, and ensures smooth clearance. CHA fees are typically ₹5,000-20,000 per shipment.

Is export from India subject to GST?

Indian exports are 0% GST — but you must have a valid LUT (Letter of Undertaking) or pay IGST and claim refund. Talk to your CA for the right approach. The Commercial Invoice must show "Supply cover Seas / Export without payment of integrated tax" to claim this.
